# Competitive teardown: Foodnome → COOK Connect / COOK Alliance

_What the closest analog to ForkFork actually became — and what it should change
for us. Researched 2026-08-01. Companion to `../GTM.md`._

**One-line takeaway:** the most successful MEHKO marketplace stopped being a
marketplace. It became a **nonprofit movement** that trains cooks (8-week
academy), gives them **$3,000 grants**, handles their permitting **for free**,
and lobbies to legalize home cooking in more places — and the checkout is just
one feature of that. That reframes both who we compete with and what "winning"
even means.

---

## The 10 findings that should move us

### 1. Verification is THE demand unlock — now confirmed by every source.
Across the whole category, the single recurring conclusion: _"most consumers
don't want to buy food from an uncertified kitchen."_ Josephine (3,000+ cooks)
died precisely because it ran an "Uber model" without health-department blessing.
**Our county-verification bet isn't a nice differentiator — it's the thing that
makes the demand side possible at all.** → _Lean into it even harder in buyer
copy; it's our strongest asset, not a footnote._

### 2. We have the chef-friendliest economics in the entire category — by a lot.
| Platform | Take | From whom |
|---|---|---|
| **ForkFork** | 8% + $0.30 | **buyer only — chef keeps 100%** |
| Hotplate | ~0% (Stripe only) | chef absorbs/passes Stripe |
| Foodnome (historic) | 15% + **$1,000 onboarding** | customer + cook |
| DishDivvy | 15% | cook |
| Josephine | 10% | cook |
| WoodSpoon | ~15% + fees | cook |
| Industry "standard" | 15–25% | cook |
"Fair payouts matter enormously for platform retention." **Chefs keeping 100% is
a real recruiting wedge — use it explicitly.**

### 3. …but nobody has made marketplace fees alone sustain a for-profit. This is the hard truth.
Foodnome raised $1.1M, charged 15% **and** a $1,000 onboarding fee, and _still_
became a nonprofit. Shef raised $9M and pivoted to a commercial-kitchen
workaround. DishDivvy folded. **Implication for you: decide the sustainability
model now.** Realistic options: (a) run lean as a mission/lifestyle business
(fee just covers costs), (b) add **services revenue** (permitting concierge —
see #4), or (c) treat fees as a long game that only works at real density. Don't
assume 8% × small GMV ever pays the bills on its own.

### 4. The real supply engine isn't _harvesting_ permits — it's _creating_ them. (Biggest product idea.)
Foodnome/COOK Connect **handle all county paperwork for the cook, for free**, then
walk them through the kitchen inspection to a permit. COOK Academy is an **8-week
course** (MEHKO rules, menu pricing, branding) whose graduates get a **$3,000
grant** + college credit. **We currently only serve the 487 people who already
have a permit. The bigger pool is the thousands who _would_ if someone held their
hand.** A "permit concierge" (we help you get MEHKO-approved, then you're on
ForkFork) would (a) expand supply beyond the fixed 487, (b) create deep loyalty
(we started your business), (c) be a plausible revenue line. → _Strong candidate
for a v2 supply play._

### 5. The killer recruiting stat: a home restaurant costs <$1,000 to start.
COOK Alliance's own number: **<$1,000 to launch a home restaurant vs. $400,000
for a brick-and-mortar vs. $50,000 for a food truck.** That's the single most
persuasive line for recruiting a cook who's ever dreamed of "opening a place."
→ _Put it in the /sell pitch and every recruiting conversation._

### 6. Retention — not acquisition — is what actually kills these businesses.
Why home food businesses fail (per the research, not bad food): _"doing skilled
work for minimum wage, managing orders through DMs, selling at the wrong market,
and burning out before the business has time to grow."_ Warning signs: selling
out every time (underpriced), ingredient cost >40% of price, not paying
themselves. **What this means for us:**
- Give chefs **pricing guidance** so they don't underprice into burnout (we
  already show tax + fee math; add "here's what similar dishes charge").
- Our order/inventory/tax tooling _is_ the "build simple systems early" that
  survivors have — market it as such.
- Actually drive demand (a chef with no orders quits fastest).

### 7. Don't become "Uber Eats 2.0." Keep it human.
Explicit category lesson: _"as platforms scale, there's a real risk they start
feeling more like Uber Eats 2.0 than a cozy marketplace, with the personal touch
and story behind each dish getting lost."_ Our "Meet the chef" already leans this
way. WoodSpoon built **chef backstory videos** for trust; that's worth copying.
→ _The warm, story-driven, local feel is a moat, not a phase to grow out of._

### 8. Partner with Santa Clara County Environmental Health — don't just scrape their list.
Every survivor partnered with health departments; Josephine died fighting them.
The county **runs the MEHKO program, owns the list, and talks to every permit
holder.** Being their friendly, compliant marketplace partner could mean
referrals, legitimacy, and a relationship no competitor has. → _Introduce
yourself to SCC DEH as a partner, not a scraper._

### 9. The mission/demographic story is a real asset (press, partnerships, grants).
Home cooks are **84% women, 48% Black or Latino, 30% first-generation
immigrants.** COOK Alliance built an entire movement — and its funding — on that
story. Positioning ForkFork as the local champion of Santa Clara's immigrant and
women home chefs unlocks press, community-org partnerships, and possibly grant
money that pure "food app" framing never would.

### 10. Santa Clara specifically is NOT densely served — the local opening is real.
COOK Connect's actual beachhead was **Riverside** (150+ cooks); in the Bay Area
they only ever claimed the "first MEHKO," no density. Shef uses a commercial-
kitchen workaround (not true home kitchens). cookin.com has _some_ SJ presence
(e.g., 40 Acres Soul Kitchen). **No one owns dense, verified, hyperlocal Santa
Clara home-cook discovery. That's still open.** But respect the incumbent: COOK
Connect is a beloved, well-connected nonprofit — **beat them on local density and
the slickest product for _this_ county, not on mission alone.**

---

## What I'd actually change

- **Now:** put the "<$1,000 to start a real food business" line + the "keep 100%"
  economics front-and-center in `/sell` and all recruiting. Reach out to SCC
  Environmental Health as a partner.
- **Soon:** decide the sustainability POV (§3) — it changes everything downstream.
  Add pricing guidance for chefs (§6). Lean harder into verification in buyer copy
  (§1) and chef stories/video (§7).
- **v2 bet to seriously consider:** a **permit-concierge** offering (§4) to create
  supply, not just harvest it — the survivors' actual growth engine.

## The uncomfortable question this raises
The closest, best-run version of ForkFork concluded that this is a **mission, not
a venture** — it gives cooks money rather than taking a cut, and survives on
grants and goodwill. Worth deciding honestly: **are you building a business, or a
movement?** The lean build means you _can_ run it as either. But the answer
shapes the fee model, the fundraising (grants vs. investors), and how you spend
the next year.
